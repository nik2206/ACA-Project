## X1. Constant-power sweep: does race-to-halt survive as π₀ → 0?

ε_flop, ε_mem, peak flop/s and peak bandwidth held at their fitted values; only π₀ changes. Race-to-halt holds while B̂_ε < B_τ. Threshold π₀* = (ε_mem/B_τ − ε_flop)/τ_flop.

| Setting | fitted π₀ (W) | idle (W) | threshold π₀* (W) | B̂_ε at π₀ = idle | B̂_ε at π₀ = 0 (= B_ε) | B̂_ε at fitted π₀ (check) | B_τ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| turbo on | 23.24 | 2.86 | none (B_ε < B_τ) | 6.16 | 6.43 | 5.35 | 9.03 |
| turbo off | 7.89 | 2.71 | 2.22 | 4.33 | 6.28 | 3.34 | 4.53 |

Turbo on has no threshold: B_ε (6.43) < B_τ (9.03), so even with π₀ = 0 the arch line reaches half its peak before the roofline corner, the situation paper 1 describes for its Intel CPU (§V-B). Turbo off flips below π₀* ≈ 2.2 W, which is below its idle package power (2.71 W): the constant power would have to fall below what the chip draws doing nothing.

### (b) Turbo off as a real power bound

Turbo off never drew more than 18.4 W in Exp 1. The turbo-on capped model cannot reach that bound at all: its constant power alone is 23.24 W, so the required Δπ would be -4.9 W (negative). The nearest feasible case in the table, Δπ/8 (bound 28.7 W), is still above the turbo-off power and is slower and far less efficient than what turbo off actually achieved. Paper 2's cap delays operations at fixed energy per operation with π₁ fixed; the real CPU met a lower power by moving to a lower frequency and voltage, which cut ε_flop, ε_mem and π₀ together (all about 3×). Paper 2 suspected the same mechanism on the Arndale GPU (§V-C).

## X3. Tiling as a work–communication trade-off, and turbo under the energy-delay product

### (a) Greenup condition (paper 1 eq. 10) applied to tiling

Baseline = naive matmul (W, Q); new = tiled (f·W, Q/m) with f = 1 (same flops). Paper 1's condition for a greenup (stated for π₀ = 0): f < 1 + ((m − 1)/m)·B_ε/I, with I the baseline intensity. With f = 1 the condition always holds; the bound is the extra work the tiled version could afford and still save energy.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| turbo on | 32 | 3.4 | 1.28 | 4.5 | 1.38 | 1.11 | 1.78 |
| turbo on | 64 | 7.3 | 1.28 | 5.3 | 1.81 | 1.25 | 2.50 * |
| turbo on | 128 | 14.8 | 1.28 | 5.7 | 1.78 | 1.28 | 2.90 * |
| turbo off | 32 | 3.4 | 1.07 | 5.1 | 2.84 | 1.64 | 1.26 |
| turbo off | 64 | 8.6 | 1.07 | 6.2 | 2.77 | 1.54 | 1.80 |
| turbo off | 128 | 17.1 | 1.07 | 6.5 | 3.11 | 1.88 | 2.17 |

\* every repeat thermally throttled (turbo on, b = 64 and 128).

For a baseline already compute-bound in time (I ≥ B_τ), paper 1's limit is f < 1 + B_ε/B_τ: turbo on 1.71, turbo off 2.39. On our turbo-on machine, an algorithm that is already fast may spend at most 71% more flops to remove all its DRAM traffic and still save energy; with turbo off, 139%.
